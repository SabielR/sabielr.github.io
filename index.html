Meet Sabiel

Biography

Sabiel Rodriguez is a Boston-based operations and go-to-market leader whose work spans public policy, regulatory strategy, and business growth.

He began his career in legal services, managing cases for more than 170 individuals seeking Social Security disability benefits. The role demanded rigor, empathy, and a practical understanding of how complex public systems affect the people who depend on them.

Sabiel later entered Massachusetts state government, where he worked in public oversight and accountability and contributed to the regulatory development of one of the nation's most closely watched emerging industries. These experiences gave him a firsthand understanding of how laws become operational policies, and how regulatory decisions shape organizations, markets, and communities.

Ahead of the 2024 presidential election, Sabiel served with the governing body responsible for administering elections in DeKalb County, Georgia, one of the country's most electorally significant counties. His work focused on expanding equitable access to the ballot, including ensuring that voters could participate regardless of the language they spoke at home.

Sabiel earned his master's degree in public policy from Georgetown University's McCourt School of Public Policy, where he was named a National Urban Fellow. He also holds a Bachelor of Arts in Political Science from Providence College and a Certificate in Regulatory Affairs for Cannabis Control from Clark University, which he completed with a 4.0 GPA.

Today, Sabiel works in the private sector, leading operations, market-entry strategy, and regulatory expansion. Drawing on experience inside government agencies and a detailed understanding of statutes and administrative systems, he brings a regulator's perspective to business growth. His approach combines strategic execution with an understanding of how oversight bodies evaluate risk, compliance, and market readiness.

Sabiel lives in Boston and writes about public policy, politics, markets, and regulated industries. When he is not thinking about how governments and public institutions work, he can usually be found spending time by the water, often with his goldendoodle, Logan, by his side.
